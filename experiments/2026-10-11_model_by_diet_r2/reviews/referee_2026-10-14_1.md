## Major concerns (each could change a conclusion)

**1. The experiment has not concluded, and the deciding criterion is drifting.**
- NOTEBOOK.md:984 Conclusions is empty. Status is "awaiting review" and Dates has no end. Every verdict is "proposed" (:390-400).
- Later, the human's stated goal is evaluation-module scores, with field recovery a "nice-to-have" (:591-596). The pre-registered bar, however, gives linear-cls equal weight (:26-31).
- If the final choice of default is made on the new goal, the aim has changed. Per EXPERIMENT_PRINCIPLES §2 that means a new experiment, or at minimum an explicit statement that the pre-registered verdict is "not met" and the choice is made outside the aim.
- **What would address it:** write Conclusions with one verdict each for the problem, H1 and H2, judged against :26-65 as written. Record any goal change as a dated decision, not a re-reading of the bar.

**2. The fallback choice depends on which control reading is used, and the tie-break isn't applied.**
- The proposed fallback (:398) picks `single_mixed` (-0.036) and `single_contrastive` (-0.038) as tied, both against control seed 0.
- Against the three-seed mean, which the Method says is reported too (:357-361), `probe_vs_control.csv` gives multi_mixed -0.046, single_mixed -0.053 and single_contrastive -0.055. So multi_mixed loses least, and all three are within 0.02 of each other.
- The control seeds alone spread 0.035 on linear-cls (0.653-0.688), which is larger than the gaps being ranked.
- The tie-break rule (:37-39: mixed beats single-objective, then single beats multi) is never applied.
- It is also unclear what "the untrained whitened encoder" in the fallback (:40-42) means: the untrained 256-d model, or the head-free 768-d whitened mean? They differ a lot (linear-cls 0.67 vs 0.86).
- **What would address it:** report the fallback under both readings, apply the tie-break explicitly, and define the default artifact.

**3. The H2 verdict is reported in one direction only, and its evidence is under the noise that matters.**
- :400 says "refuted, in single-card's favour", from a 0.030 deck-AUC gap on contrastive.
- The pre-registered refutation is "either model leads by 0.02 or more ... in either direction" (:62-65). The metric-diet gap is 0.022 in multi-card's favour (0.5215 vs 0.4997, `probe_summary.csv`). It is set aside after the fact as "carries little weight".
- Read as written, H2 is refuted in both directions, which really means "diet-dependent / not resolvable at one seed".
- "The bootstrap intervals don't overlap" (:400, :653-654) resamples decks. It says nothing about training-seed variance, which was never measured. Control-seed spread on deck AUC (0.567-0.578) covers random initialization only, not training noise.
- The comparison is also not single-variable. Single-card has 9 MLP blocks; multi-card has 3 blocks plus 2 attention layers run on a group of one in the decision view. Parameter counts are matched (2,898,176 vs 2,895,616, in the bench notes only), but depth and an out-of-distribution length-one attention input are not.
- **What would address it:** reword to "H2 refuted by the 0.02 criterion on two diets in opposite directions; one seed; pilot". Add the parameter counts and the depth difference to the Method's confounds. Seeds of `single_contrastive` and `multi_contrastive` (already proposed as E) are needed before any model claim.

**4. The fresh-head "mixed transfers best" claim (finding 6, :434-439; Results :770-779) sits inside noise the folder itself measures.**
- The held-out fresh-head runs use one control seed (s0) and one head seed, and count a dojo better or worse at a 0.02 threshold.
- The Gwent fresh-head runs, which have three control seeds, show how large the noise is:
  - per-dojo control spread is up to 0.22 (`gwent_one.color_mask` single 0.45-0.67, `extrinsic_gwent_validation.csv`);
  - median spread is 0.08 (single 0.78-0.86).
- `control_single_s0` alone sits at -0.0165 median and 5/2 better/worse against the control mean (`extrinsic_gwent_summary.csv`). That is about the same size as single_mixed's -0.047 and 7/4 on the held-out set.
- Finding 6 headlines the unpaired medians (0.856 / 0.814 against 0.938 / 0.915). By that same measure single_contrastive (0.876) also beats its control, yet the text calls contrastive "no clear median difference" using the paired statistic. The two statistics are mixed selectively.
- **What would address it:** rewording to "no difference distinguishable from initialization noise" is enough. Existing data allows one quick check: rerun `extrinsic_held_out.py` with control seeds 1 and 2 (minutes each with the feature cache), and report paired differences against the control mean, as the Gwent analysis already does.

**5. The fresh-head budget measures early learning speed, not what the encoder supports.**
- `best_test_round` is 5, the last round, for most dojos and encoders (`extrinsic_validation.csv`, `extrinsic_gwent_validation.csv`; e.g. every leader and hero dojo). The heads were still improving when stopped, at about 230 steps per dojo.
- Encoders with very different scale and anisotropy (mean cosine 0.54-0.99) may simply train a fresh head at different speeds under a fixed lr of 1e-3.
- This affects finding 6 and the Gwent claim "The deck skill transfers to Gwent once a head is trained" (:919-928). That claim also has a competing reading: contrastive-trained embeddings give pooled deck vectors a fresh head fits faster. Gwent leaders are tied to factions, and faction appears verbatim in the card JSON (:585-586).
- **What would address it:** rewording ("within a 3,000-step head budget"). The convincing fix is a longer head budget on the leader and hero dojos, and on the 4 dojos nobody learns.

**6. Finding 7, "Small per-card statistic dojos overfit" (:440-442, :692-695), is not supported.**
- `test_loss_by_dojo.csv`: `multi_metric` `sts_gg.card_deck_size` is 2.14 at round 0 and 2.43 at round 3; `sts2_runs.card_relic_count` is 1.97 then 1.93. These dojos sit at about 2× the mean predictor from the first evaluation.
- Training loss was not logged (the notebook says so at :528).
- The pattern fits just as well with a TRAIN/TEST label-distribution shift for per-card statistics across card tiers, or a baseline/normalization mismatch.
- **What would address it:** reword to "end worse than the mean predictor from round 0; cause not determined". Overfitting needs training loss, or a TRAIN-split evaluation of the saved checkpoints (existing checkpoints).

**7. The "transfer" readings of the deck-level held-out dojos and the non-TRAIN anchor split may be in-sample at the deck level.**
- "FaB hero from deck: 0.24-0.29 against 0.92-1.01" (:424-425, :783-785) is presented as a held-out deck-level gain. But contrastive.flesh_and_blood trains on FaB decks, and the metric diet trains `fabtcg_decklists.hero_conditioned_inclusion` (configs/single_mixed.yaml:52).
- If the hero dojo's VALIDATION decks overlap the decks contrastive trained on, this is not transfer. The folder doesn't state whether deck splits are shared across dojos built from the same source.
- The anchor-tier split (:739-743, `deck_auc_nontrain_anchors` 0.725) splits by card tier. The anchor's deck-mates and the deck itself may be TRAIN material. So "the signal holds for non-TRAIN-tier cards" shows generalization to unseen anchor cards only if non-TRAIN cards are guaranteed absent from contrastive training examples.
- The notebook concedes the probe decks are "partly TRAIN decks" (:495-496) but still uses these numbers as out-of-sample evidence.
- **What would address it:** state in the Method how deck splits and card tiers interact for contrastive and deck-level dojos (cite the source file). Report deck AUC on non-TRAIN anchors within non-TRAIN decks, if the probe pool permits (existing data).

**8. The slice part of the bar (Gwent deck AUC) has no uncertainty, and its deltas are near the control spread.**
- "Gwent deck AUC is lower by 0.01 to 0.09" (:735) rests on 25 decks with no interval (`probe_deck_by_game.csv`). Control seeds span 0.581-0.612 on Gwent.
- For single_contrastive (0.568, -0.014 vs s0) the gap is inside plausible noise.
- The problem verdict survives anyway, because slice linear-cls fails by 0.09-0.20. But "every cell is below its controls on both measures" overstates the Gwent half.
- **What would address it:** add a bootstrap interval for Gwent (the script already bootstraps the full probe), and reword.

- **"Multi-card metric-only ... far worse than chance (3.70, 3.81). This fits the collapsed geometry" (:977-978) doesn't follow.** A collapsed embedding predicts about chance, as `single_metric` shows (cosine 0.99, zero-shot about 1.00). Confidently wrong scores point to something else, likely the attention over groups.
- **Provenance gap.** The `single_mixed` bench note gives commit ced7ba7 with no "code matches 467d0e1" check, unlike the other single-card runs. single_mixed is a candidate default, so state whether training code changed.
- **Post-hoc analysis choices.**
  - The slice was changed from the probe pool to all cards of three games (:380-383).
  - The controls were changed from matched-init to three random seeds (:354-361).
  - Both are said to be decided "before the fresh-head results", not before the probe results that the bar uses. State which.
- **Unbalanced slice.** Slice linear-cls is an unweighted mean over three fields, one with n=101 (STS2 type). Per-field values are in `probe_by_field.csv`, but the summary should say so.

The paper should say linear-cls measures preservation of input-text facts, not "card information" in general. Finding 5's framing, "loses the most information of all", should carry that scope.
- **Cosine-based metrics are confounded by anisotropy.** Deck AUC, kNN, nearest-centroid and NMI are compared across encoders whose mean cosine runs from 0.53 to 0.99 (untrained single-card already 0.81). A centered-cosine re-run of the intrinsic suite on existing embeddings would show how much of the "collapse" and cluster differences is offset.
- **Pokémon memorization (:473-474) is speculation the folder can check.** Report per-game TRAIN vs non-TRAIN anchor AUC.
- **Recipe is stale and data is undescribed (paper and recipe tests).**
  - The Recipe (:215-218) still describes the probe as a script "written after training" and lists none of the 10 `analysis/` scripts or their order.
  - Hardware and library versions (RX 6800, torch 2.10.0+rocm7.13) are in the bench notes only.
  - The configs point to per-run `whitening_stats.pt` copies, while the Method names `data/whitening/...`. The byte-identity claim is in the Log only.
  - Data sizes (decks per game, TRAIN examples per dojo) are absent.
- **Trainable parameter counts (2,898,176 / 2,895,616) belong in the Method**, which currently says "--check reports the exact counts" (:180).
- **Shared-variable diet confound not named.**
  - Mixed gets half the contrastive steps and half the metric steps of the single-objective cells. "Contrastive-only beats mixed on deck AUC" (H1, :399) can't separate "metric harms" from "less contrastive exposure".
  - The diet is the variable, so this is inherent, but it should be named for the H1 mechanism claim ("metric-only collapses"). The variant that separates them is contrastive-only at 10k steps.
- **Ranking at a fixed budget while still improving.** Every cell was still improving at 20k steps (bench notes), so the ranking applies to this budget only. Per-round checkpoints exist: probing them would show whether the deck-AUC vs linear-cls trade-off is monotone (existing data).

## Suggestions (not required)

- An untrained-head offline sweep (proposed A): a random linear 768→256 projection vs the residual MLP. This separates width from depth/LayerNorm for finding 5.
- Contrastive-only at 10k steps, to separate the diet effect from contrastive exposure.
- A centered-cosine version of deck AUC and nearest-centroid.
- Related work to consider citing:
  - Ethayarajh 2019 (anisotropy of contextual embeddings) and Su et al. 2021 (BERT-whitening, arXiv:2103.15316), for the whitening premise;
  - Hua et al. 2021, "On Feature Decorrelation in Self-Supervised Learning", alongside VICReg/Jing et al. for collapse;
  - Kornblith et al. 2019, "Do Better ImageNet Models Transfer Better?", on linear-probe vs fine-tune transfer evaluation;
  - Hewitt & Liang 2019 (control tasks for probes), relevant to linear-cls recovering literal input fields.
